**Version 1.0 — 2026-08-13**

>
> The mmCal source code is open source under the BSD 3-Clause License. You may use it, modify it, redistribute it, include it in commercial products, and sell products or services that use it, subject to that license.
>
> The **mmCal name and official logos are different**: they identify the upstream mmCal project. Third-party products are welcome, including commercial GUIs, wrappers, ports, integrations, and forks, but they must use their **own primary product name and visual identity** and must not look official, endorsed, certified, or released by the mmCal Project.

---

## 1. Purpose

mmCal is intentionally distributed under a permissive open-source license. The project welcomes personal use, commercial use, modification, redistribution, integration, packaging, and independent products built on top of mmCal.

Open-source freedom does not require that unrelated products share the same identity.

This policy therefore separates:

1. **the software**, licensed under the BSD 3-Clause License; and
2. **the mmCal brand**, which identifies the upstream mmCal project and its official releases.

The purpose of this policy is not to prevent competition, commercial use, forks, GUIs, or paid products. Its purpose is to prevent confusion about **source, affiliation, endorsement, certification, and official status**.

> **You may build on mmCal. You may sell what you build. You may accurately say that it uses mmCal. You may not make it look as though your product is mmCal itself or is officially endorsed by the mmCal Project when it is not.**

---

## 2. Relationship to the BSD 3-Clause License

The BSD 3-Clause License governs copyright permissions for the mmCal software.

This trademark policy is separate. It does not reduce or revoke the copyright permissions granted by the BSD 3-Clause License.

This policy does **not** prohibit:

- commercial use of the code;
- selling copies of the software;
- commercial redistribution;
- proprietary software that incorporates mmCal where the BSD 3-Clause License permits it;
- commercial GUIs, wrappers, launchers, frontends, integrations, or services using mmCal;
- forks or modified versions.

Those activities remain subject to the BSD 3-Clause License and applicable law.

This policy only addresses use of the **mmCal Marks** as branding or source identifiers.

Nothing in the BSD 3-Clause License should be read as permission to falsely imply endorsement by the mmCal Project. Its non-endorsement clause also remains independently applicable.

---

## 3. The mmCal Marks

For purposes of this policy, the **“mmCal Marks”** means, to the extent controlled by the mmCal Project:

- the name **mmCal** when used to identify the project, software, or official distributions;
- official stylized forms of the mmCal name;
- official mmCal logos, icons, and wordmarks published by the upstream project;
- any other brand element that the upstream project explicitly identifies as an official mmCal mark.

This definition describes the scope of this policy. It is not a representation that every listed mark is registered in every jurisdiction.

Unless the project expressly states otherwise, do **not** use the `®` symbol with an mmCal Mark.

For this policy, **“mmCal Project”** means the upstream mmCal project and the person or entity authorized to control the mmCal Marks.

---

## 4. Fundamental rule: no confusion about official status

Any use of an mmCal Mark must not be reasonably likely to make users believe that a third-party product, service, website, package, organization, event, or account is:

- an official mmCal release;
- operated by the mmCal Project;
- sponsored by the mmCal Project;
- certified by the mmCal Project;
- approved by the mmCal Project;
- endorsed by the mmCal Project;
- affiliated with the mmCal Project when no such affiliation exists.

This rule applies even to uses otherwise permitted by this policy.

Words such as the following require written permission when used to imply project approval:

- `official`
- `certified`
- `approved`
- `endorsed`
- `authorized`
- `the official GUI`
- `the official frontend`
- `official distribution`

# Part I — Uses that do not require permission

## 5. Truthful descriptive references

You may use **mmCal** to truthfully refer to the project or software.

Examples:

- `This application uses mmCal.`
- `Powered by mmCal.`
- `Built with mmCal.`
- `Compatible with mmCal.`
- `A frontend for mmCal.`
- `A plugin for mmCal.`
- `Based on mmCal 1.5.1.`
- `Derived from the mmCal source code.`
- `Includes an unmodified copy of mmCal.`
- `Supports mmCal expression syntax.`

The statement must be factual and must not imply sponsorship or official status.

Where ambiguity is possible, use a disclaimer such as:

> This is an independent third-party project and is not an official mmCal release or endorsed by the mmCal Project.

---

## 6. Articles, reviews, books, research, commentary, and criticism

You may use the mmCal name in:

- articles, reviews, and books;
- academic papers;
- technical documentation and tutorials;
- benchmark reports;
- videos and presentations;
- news reporting;
- commentary, criticism, and historical discussion.

You may use screenshots of official mmCal software when reasonably necessary to discuss or document mmCal, subject to applicable copyright law.

No permission is required merely to state facts, compare mmCal with another product, criticize mmCal, or discuss the project.

Do not format an independent work in a way that makes it look like an official mmCal publication.

Examples:

- **Allowed:** `Understanding the mmCal Evaluator`
- **Allowed:** `A Benchmark of mmCal, Eigenmath, and SymPy`
- **Allowed:** `Unofficial Guide to mmCal`
- **Not allowed without permission:** `The Official mmCal Manual` for an independently published book

---

## 7. Linking to the official project

You may use **mmCal** in ordinary text links to the upstream project, official documentation, or official distribution pages.

A small unmodified official logo may also be used solely as a link to the official project when the context makes clear that the logo identifies the destination, not the third-party site itself.

Do not use the logo as the third party's own site logo or identity.

---

## 8. Unmodified redistribution of official releases

You may redistribute an **unmodified official mmCal release** under the name `mmCal`, including commercially, provided that:

1. it is genuinely an unmodified official release;
2. all BSD 3-Clause notices and other required notices are preserved;
3. the upstream version is accurately identified where practical;
4. you do not imply that your redistribution channel is operated or endorsed by the mmCal Project;
5. you do not alter the software, logo, version information, or release identity while continuing to present it as an official upstream binary.

You may charge money for distribution, media, support, installation, or related services. Charging money does not by itself make the use improper.

Recommended wording:

> Includes an unmodified copy of mmCal. mmCal is an independent open-source project; this distributor is not affiliated with the mmCal Project.

---

## 9. Operating-system and package-manager distributions

Operating-system distributions and package repositories may use `mmcal` or `mmCal` as the package name for a good-faith package of upstream mmCal without separate permission, provided that:

- the package substantially represents upstream mmCal;
- changes are limited to ordinary packaging, portability, build-system, security, or distribution-integration work and do not intentionally create a materially different mathematical product;
- patches are documented in the normal manner of the distribution;
- the package does not claim to be an upstream official package unless it actually is;
- the distribution's own identity is clear.

If a package materially changes mmCal's mathematical semantics, language, behavior, or product identity, it should use a distinct name under the fork rules below.

This exception does not grant arbitrary third parties a right to capture the bare `mmcal` identifier on every software registry. Where a bare registry name normally implies upstream ownership, coordinate with the mmCal Project before claiming it.

---

## 10. Source-hosting forks

Ordinary source-hosting forks are permitted.

If a hosting platform automatically preserves the repository name `mmCal` as part of its fork mechanism, that technical behavior alone is not considered improper branding.

However:

- the fork must not falsely claim to be upstream;
- a fork that becomes an independent project should adopt a distinct project name;
- releases from a materially modified fork must follow Section 14;
- an independent fork should not use the official mmCal logo as its own project avatar or primary identity.

Where confusion is possible, a statement such as `Unofficial fork of mmCal` is encouraged.

---

## 11. Compatibility, adapters, bindings, and plugins

Third-party compatibility tools may use `mmCal` descriptively.

Examples:

- `Acme Adapter for mmCal`
- `FooMath mmCal compatibility module`
- `acme-mmcal-adapter`
- `Bar bindings for mmCal`

A technical package or module identifier may contain `mmcal` when it clearly identifies an adapter, binding, integration, or compatibility component rather than pretending to be upstream mmCal itself.

Prefer the third-party identity first:

- `acme-mmcal`
- `foo-mmcal-adapter`

Avoid a bare `mmcal` identifier for an unrelated third-party implementation.

---

## 12. Independent community groups

Independent, noncommercial community groups may use the mmCal name descriptively if their independent status is clear.

Examples:

- `Tokyo mmCal Users Group — independent community group`
- `mmCal Users Japan (Unofficial)`

Such groups should have their own visual identity and should not use the official mmCal logo as though they were an official local chapter without permission.

# Part II — Third-party products, forks, GUIs, and commercial uses

## 13. Commercial use is welcome

Commercial activity is not prohibited.

You may:

- sell software that uses mmCal;
- sell a GUI for mmCal;
- sell a commercial integration;
- use mmCal inside proprietary software where permitted by BSD 3-Clause;
- sell support, hosting, installation, consulting, or training;
- redistribute mmCal for a fee;
- create a paid service powered by mmCal.

The key requirement is **independent branding**.

Commercial use does not create a right to brand a third-party product as `mmCal`.

---

## 14. Modified versions and forks must have their own primary name

A materially modified version or independent fork must use a **distinct primary product/project name** unless written permission has been granted.

The mmCal name may still be used descriptively to explain lineage.

### Good examples

- `ExactFox — based on mmCal`
- `ExactFox, derived from mmCal 1.5.1`
- `Numerica — built on the mmCal engine`
- `FooCAS — an mmCal-derived symbolic calculator`

### Not permitted without written permission

- `mmCal Pro`
- `mmCal Plus`
- `mmCal NG`
- `mmCal Next`
- `mmCal Community Edition`
- `mmCal Enterprise`
- `mmCal Extended`
- `mmCal++`
- `mmCal X`
- any name whose dominant impression is that the third-party product is a new official mmCal edition

A fork may truthfully preserve attribution and version lineage, but must not preserve official release branding in a way that makes it indistinguishable from upstream.

### Executable and internal identifiers

Where reasonably practical, materially modified distributions should also use a distinct executable, package, or application identifier.

If compatibility requires retaining a technical identifier such as `mmcal`, the user-visible product name, version output, documentation, and distribution page must still make the third-party status clear.

Third-party GUIs and wrappers are explicitly welcome, including commercial ones.

They must have:

- their own primary product name;
- their own icon and visual identity;
- clear wording describing their relationship to mmCal.

### Allowed without permission

- `Numerica — a GUI for mmCal`
- `CalcDesk — frontend for mmCal`
- `ExactShell — powered by mmCal`
- `Acme Math Studio — includes mmCal`
- app-store title: `Numerica — GUI for mmCal`

- `mmCal GUI`

- `mmCal Studio`
- `mmCal Workbench`

- making the splash screen, installer, store listing, or website look like an upstream mmCal release

A third-party GUI may bundle an unmodified `mmcal` executable. That does not make the GUI an official mmCal product.

Recommended notice:

> This application is an independent third-party frontend for mmCal. It is not an official mmCal release and is not endorsed by the mmCal Project.

# Part III — Name, logo, domains, and presentation

## 19. Product and organization names

Do not use `mmCal`, a confusingly similar variant, or an mmCal logo as the primary name or identity of an unrelated:

- company;
- organization;
- commercial product;
- independent software project;
- hosted service;
- application;
- store;
- certification program.

Descriptive uses remain permitted.

The model is:

`OWN BRAND — relationship to mmCal`

not:

`mmCal — third party's edition`

---

## 20. Domains, social accounts, and organization handles

Without written permission, do not register or use a domain, organization name, or account handle whose overall presentation is likely to appear official or appropriate the mmCal identity.

Examples normally requiring permission:

- `mmcal-official.example`
- `officialmmcal.example`
- `mmcalproject.example` for an unrelated site
- `@mmcal_official`
- `@official_mmcal`
- an app-store publisher named `mmCal Project` that is not upstream

Descriptive uses under a clearly independent domain are generally acceptable:

- `acme.example/products/mmcal-adapter`
- `docs.acme.example/mmcal-integration`
- `mmcal.acme.example` where it is clearly an Acme integration page

When in doubt, use your own brand in the registrable domain and use `mmCal` in a path, subtitle, or descriptive text.

## 21. Search metadata and advertising

You may use `mmCal` as a truthful keyword to describe compatibility, support, or integration.

Do not:

- present advertising in a way that falsely suggests it is from the mmCal Project;
- use misleading phrases such as `official mmCal download`;

## 22. Logo use

Official mmCal logos are more likely than plain text to imply sponsorship or official status, so logo use is stricter.

### Normally allowed without separate permission

An unmodified official logo may be used:

- in an article or presentation discussing mmCal;
- in documentation identifying mmCal as a dependency;
- in a comparison chart identifying the official project;
- as a small link to the official project;

Do not:

- use the official mmCal logo as your application's icon;
- use it as your company's or project's logo;
- incorporate it into your own logo;
- create a confusingly similar derived logo for an independent fork;
- place it prominently on packaging or an app-store listing so the product appears official;
- use it as a certification or approval seal;
- materially distort, rotate, redraw, or combine the logo with another brand to create a new source identifier.

Simple technical resizing is acceptable when proportions are preserved.

If official logo variants or brand assets are published, use those rather than recreating the logo.

# Part IV — Modified software and technical identity

## 25. What counts as a materially modified version?

A modification is likely to be **material** for branding purposes when it intentionally changes one or more of the following in a way users may reasonably attribute to mmCal itself:

- mathematical semantics;
- parser or language behavior;
- exact/approximate evaluation rules;
- branch conventions;
- supported functions in a way marketed as a distinct edition;
- solver or simplifier behavior;
- security or trust characteristics;
- user-visible product identity.

Routine packaging and portability changes are generally not material when they preserve upstream behavior.

Materiality depends on actual effect, not merely the number of changed lines.

When uncertain, using a distinct product name is the safest and preferred approach.

---

## 26. Attribution for derivatives

BSD 3-Clause notice requirements apply independently.

For a derivative product, the mmCal Project additionally recommends clear wording such as:

> Based on mmCal. This is an independent derivative work and is not an official mmCal release.

or:

> Contains software derived from mmCal. The mmCal Project does not endorse or certify this product.

This policy does not require anyone to hide the relationship to mmCal. Accurate attribution is encouraged.

---

## 27. Version references

You may accurately refer to upstream versions:

- `compatible with mmCal 1.5.1`
- `based on mmCal 1.5.1`
- `includes mmCal 1.5.1`
- `forked from mmCal v1.5.1`

Do not invent an official-looking mmCal version number for an independent derivative, such as:

- `mmCal 1.5.1 Pro`
- `mmCal 2.0 Enterprise`

without written permission.

# Part V — Uses requiring written permission

## 28. Written permission is required for

Unless otherwise allowed above, obtain written permission before:

1. using `mmCal` as the primary name of a third-party product or service;
2. naming a fork or modified release as though it were an mmCal edition;
3. using the official logo as an application icon or primary product identity;
4. creating a derived logo intended to function as a source identifier;
5. using `official`, `certified`, `approved`, `endorsed`, or equivalent wording that implies project approval;
6. creating a certification, accreditation, or authorized-partner program using the mmCal Marks;
7. selling merchandise whose main branding/value is an mmCal Mark;
8. operating a commercial event whose primary event name is an mmCal Mark;
9. operating a domain/account likely to be mistaken for the official project;
10. any use whose context makes official affiliation unclear and cannot be resolved by ordinary descriptive wording and a disclaimer.

Permission may include additional conditions for the specific use.

---

## 29. How to request permission

Send a written request using the contact method published by the upstream mmCal repository.

If no dedicated trademark contact is listed, contact the upstream repository owner or open an issue titled:

`Trademark permission request`

A request should include:

- your name or organization;
- proposed product, service, event, or project name;
- the exact mmCal Mark you want to use;
- where and how it will appear;
- whether the use is commercial;
- mockups or screenshots where practical;
- expected duration;
- a contact address for the response.

Permission is not granted merely because a request was submitted.

Keep a copy of written approval.

# Part VI — Enforcement, correction, and changes

## 30. Good-faith mistakes

The mmCal Project prefers correction over unnecessary confrontation.

For an ordinary good-faith violation, the project will normally attempt to identify the confusing use and request a reasonable correction.

Possible corrections include:

- changing the primary product name;
- adding an independence disclaimer;
- replacing the official logo with the third party's own icon;
- changing an app-store title;
- changing domain/account presentation;
- correcting claims of endorsement.

This preference does not waive any rights.

---

## 31. Serious deceptive uses

The project may respond without a normal cure period when a use involves:

- deliberate impersonation of the mmCal Project;
- fraud;
- malware or malicious software presented as official mmCal;
- phishing;
- deceptive collection of credentials or payments;
- counterfeit “official” distributions;
- continued confusing use after clear notice.

---

## 32. Conditional permission

Permissions provided by this policy are conditioned on compliance with this policy.

If a use falls outside the permitted conditions, the project may ask that the use be changed or discontinued.

A party that corrects a good-faith violation may resume any use independently permitted by the corrected terms of this policy.

---

## 33. Changes to this policy

The mmCal Project may update this policy as the project and brand evolve.

Each published version should include a version number and date.

Where practical, the project intends to provide a reasonable transition period for existing good-faith uses that complied with a previous published version.

No transition period is promised for fraud, impersonation, malware, or uses creating an urgent risk of user confusion.

# Part VII — Legal scope

## 34. No expansion of legal rights

This policy describes how the mmCal Project permits use of the mmCal Marks to the extent such permission is within the project's control.

It does not create trademark rights that do not otherwise exist under applicable law, does not represent that a mark is registered in a particular jurisdiction, and does not limit any use that applicable law independently permits regardless of this policy.

Depending on jurisdiction, independent legal rights may protect descriptive, nominative, comparative, journalistic, critical, or other fair uses.

Nothing in this policy should be interpreted to prohibit lawful criticism or commentary.

# Appendix C — Practical naming test

Before publishing a third-party product, ask:

1. **If a user saw only the product name and icon, would they reasonably think it came from the mmCal Project?**
2. **Is my own brand more prominent than the mmCal reference?**
3. **Am I describing a relationship to mmCal, or am I using mmCal as my product's identity?**
4. **Have I used words such as “official,” “certified,” or “approved”?**
5. **Would a short independence disclaimer remove any remaining ambiguity?**

If Question 1 is “yes,” or Question 3 indicates that mmCal is being used as the third party's own identity, choose a different primary name or request permission.
